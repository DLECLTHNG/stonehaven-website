A jumbo mortgage starts with the loan amount

A jumbo mortgage exceeds the conforming loan limit for the property’s county and unit count. A luxury home does not automatically need a jumbo loan: your down payment changes the amount borrowed. Stonehaven is a mortgage brokerage arranging high-value home financing in Alabama, South Carolina, North Carolina, Georgia, Tennessee and Florida.

For a one-unit home, the 2026 baseline conforming limit is $832,750; some counties have higher limits. Check the property’s county using the current FHFA county loan-limit table. Being within a limit does not establish eligibility. Jumbo requirements also vary by program.

Plan the purchase around the cash you want to keep

Choose a down payment after looking at closing costs, reserves, near-term commitments and the proposed monthly payment. A larger down payment reduces the loan, but also leaves less liquidity. Ask for comparable scenarios using the same property, closing date and documented income.

Hypothetical purchase: a $1.5 million home with a $300,000 down payment requires a $1.2 million loan before any other financing. If the buyer has $500,000 available and closing uses are assumed to be $40,000, $160,000 remains afterward. Increasing the down payment to $450,000 reduces the loan to $1.05 million but leaves $10,000 under those assumptions. These figures illustrate cash allocation, not a program’s minimum down payment or reserve requirement.

The jumbo down-payment and reserves guide shows how to keep the purchase budget separate from the funds needed after closing.

Match the income review to how you are paid

For a salaried buyer, start with base pay and identify bonuses, commissions or equity compensation separately. For a business owner, distinguish business revenue, expenses, ownership share and money available personally. A strong business bank balance is not automatically personal qualifying income.

If tax-return income does not describe the full picture, we can review whether a bank-statement mortgage fits the documented circumstances. That route still requires an income review and does not mean every deposit is usable income. Read the self-employed jumbo mortgage preparation guide before collecting a file.

Primary residence, second home or rental?

Tell us the intended use before discussing terms. A home you will occupy, a second home and a property primarily held for rental income can require different financing. Include any planned rental use, management agreement or existing lease. Do not choose an occupancy label just to obtain a preferred quote.

If the plan is a rental investment, start with our DSCR financing review. For a personal home, we review the residential route.

Compare payment structures beyond the opening payment

Compare fixed and adjustable structures using the expected ownership period, adjustment terms and ability to handle a higher payment. If interest-only financing is considered, identify when principal payments begin and what happens to the payment afterward.

The CFPB explains that interest-only payments do not reduce principal during the interest-only period. Our interest-only mortgage guide covers the questions to ask before choosing that structure. Availability is subject to the specific program.

Review the property before setting a firm financing timetable

For a custom home, large acreage, waterfront property or condominium, describe the property accurately at the start. Ask what valuation and property review are needed, who orders them and whether the closing date leaves time to resolve questions. Do not assume a listing price is the financeable value.

Collect an address-specific insurance quote. For a condominium, identify association dues, known assessments and the documents available from the association. These are practical preparation steps, not a statement that every property qualifies.

What to share for an initial review

  • Property state, county and expected purchase price.
  • Requested loan and approximate down payment.
  • Primary residence, second home or investment use.
  • Income type: salary, business ownership, variable compensation or other documented income.
  • Whether you are under contract, selling another home and your target closing date.

Start with a summary in the inquiry below. Keep tax returns, bank statements, account numbers and identification documents out of the public form. We explain the appropriate document process after the initial conversation.

Jumbo mortgage questions

Does a $1 million home require a jumbo mortgage?

Not necessarily. The loan amount after the down payment, county limit and unit count determine whether it exceeds the conforming limit. Other underwriting requirements still apply.

Is there one minimum down payment for every jumbo loan?

No. The loan amount, occupancy, property and borrower profile affect the available structure. Ask for the actual down payment and reserves required for the proposed loan.

Can a self-employed buyer be considered?

Yes, we can review a self-employed purchase scenario. The appropriate documentation path depends on the business, income and requested loan; review is not approval.

Can I inquire before finding a property?

Yes. Start with the state, price range, intended use and income type. A preliminary conversation helps identify what needs review before you commit to a property.
