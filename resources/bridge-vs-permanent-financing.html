Bridge vs. permanent:
which does your deal need?

One question decides it: is the property done changing?

By Christiaan De Leeuw · Stonehaven Lending · Updated July 30, 2026

The Short Answer

Permanent financing fits a stabilized, cash-flowing property. Bridge financing is short-term capital (one to three years) for properties in transition: renovation, lease-up, quick close. Bridge costs more and needs a credible exit.

Permanent

The default for stabilized assets

Real rent roll, known expenses: permanent debt gives you term and amortization. Stabilized and holding? This is the default.

Bridge

Paying for speed and flexibility

Bridge lenders underwrite the plan: what the property becomes, what it costs, how you exit. You pay more for speed, flexibility on condition and occupancy, and often renovation dollars and interest reserves.

The Exit

Underwrite your way out first

Every bridge loan is a bet on its exit. Size the takeout first: stabilized NOI, what DSCR and LTV sizing supports conservatively, and whether that retires the bridge with room. If it only works at aggressive rents, stop.

In Practice

Common patterns

Buy-fix-refinance; a fast close, refinanced once stabilized; construction-to-permanent; a maturity before the plan finishes. Each has an end date. No end date? Permanent debt, or a smaller deal.
