SBA 7(a) vs 504:
how to choose

Compare financing for the building your business will occupy, equipment you need and cash for the move.

By Chris De Leeuw · Stonehaven Lending · Updated November 7, 2026

  1. Home
  2. SBA resources
  3. SBA 7(a) vs 504 for Real Estate: How to Choose

What will your business buy with the loan?

List the building, improvements, equipment and operating cash separately. A 504 structure can finance eligible fixed assets for an operating business. A 7(a) loan can also cover eligible working capital or a business acquisition. Ask how much usable floor area your business will occupy and when it will move in.

A building purchase with a working-capital need

Consider an owner buying a $2 million building for the business and budgeting another $150,000 for inventory and payroll. Under a 504 structure, identify a separate source for the $150,000 and confirm the eligible building costs with the Certified Development Company. Ask whether a 7(a) structure could cover both uses and how the payment, collateral and cash required at closing would compare.

For a project consisting of a building and long-lived equipment, compare the bank and CDC portions of a 504 proposal with the full 7(a) proposal. Separate the borrower contribution, fees and any cash required after closing. Each structure requires its own eligibility and credit review.

What to bring to the financing review

  • Purchase price, estimated improvements and equipment costs.
  • Floor area the operating business will occupy and any tenant leases.
  • Working capital needed after the move, and cash available for closing.
  • Operating history and a schedule for purchase, improvements and occupancy.
