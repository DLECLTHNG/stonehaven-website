How lenders size
a commercial loan

Two limits set proceeds - and one is binding your deal right now.

By Christiaan De Leeuw · Stonehaven Lending · Updated July 30, 2026

The Short Answer

The loan is the lesser of two numbers: what income services at a target coverage ratio (DSCR, commonly 1.20x-1.30x), and what value supports at maximum loan-to-value (LTV). The smaller is the limiting factor.

Limit One

DSCR: income covers debt

Underwriting rebuilds NOI from the rent roll and operating statements. NOI divided by the target ratio sets allowable debt service; rate and amortization turn that into a loan amount.

Limit Two

LTV: value caps leverage

The appraisal sets value; the lender's maximum LTV sets the ceiling. When LTV binds, it is about value and equity. Income does not move an LTV-bound loan.

The Point

Work the limit that binds

Our commercial loan calculator names the limiting factor. DSCR-bound? Fight for income: rents, expenses, amortization. LTV-bound? Fight for value: appraisal, comps, or more equity.

Beyond the Two

What else shapes proceeds

Debt yield, stress tests, tenant concentration, rollover, sponsor strength sit on top - weighted differently by each lender. That spread is where a broker earns their keep.
