The short answer

A DSCR rental loan typically tests qualifying rent against the property’s payment instead of qualifying the borrower through personal-income DTI. A conventional investment-property loan evaluates documented borrower income and debts, and may also use eligible rental income. Both routes still evaluate credit, assets, the property and other program conditions.

Can conventional financing count rental income?

Yes, subject to the applicable rules. A conventional loan does not simply ignore the new property’s rent. Fannie Mae’s rental-income guidance distinguishes documentation, management experience and whether income can offset the property payment or add to qualifying income. Ask how the actual file is treated, rather than assuming all gross rent counts.

The DTI calculation compares qualifying monthly obligations with qualifying monthly income. Self-employment alone does not rule out conventional financing; the documented income and the rest of the application determine whether that route fits.

What does a DSCR ratio tell me?

For a simplified rental example, $2,500 of qualifying monthly rent divided by a $2,000 qualifying housing payment equals 1.25. If that payment rises to $2,200, the ratio falls to about 1.14. Those are arithmetic examples, not approval thresholds or loan quotes.

The $500 difference in the first example is before maintenance, vacancy, management and other operating expenses. It is not a profit guarantee. Confirm which rent the program accepts and which payment components it uses, then try the DSCR calculator.

Does a bigger portfolio automatically require DSCR?

No. Fannie Mae publishes specific financed-property counting and reserve rules. Count the relevant properties and obligations under the selected program. A third property does not automatically force an investor out of conventional financing, and DSCR does not automatically remove every exposure limit.

Give the broker an accurate property and debt summary through a secure document process. An LLC, a personal guarantee and an ownership percentage can affect different parts of review; do not use one label as a substitute for reviewing the actual obligations.

How do I compare the cost of two eligible offers?

Start with the same loan amount, closing date and expected sale or refinance date. Compare the following in writing:

  • Cash to close: down payment, points and other costs, with financed charges identified separately.
  • Cash that must remain available: required reserves and your operating budget.
  • Payment structure: fixed or adjustable rate, amortization, any interest-only period and actual maturity.
  • Exit: remaining principal, prepayment provisions and charges at the planned payoff date.
  • Unresolved conditions: appraisal, rent documentation, insurance, title and anything still required before approval.

A lower starting payment can leave more principal outstanding. Compare payments already made, remaining debt and separately paid fees at the same date. Neither product is always cheaper, and a preliminary quote is not an approval.

Prepare the file for the right comparison

Share the property state, purchase or refinance purpose, value or price, requested financing, rent, taxes, insurance and proposed ownership. Tell us whether you can document personal income and whether the property needs substantial work. A property that is not ready to rent may need a bridge or renovation route before permanent rental financing.
