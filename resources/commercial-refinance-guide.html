When to refinance
a commercial property

A refinance does one of four jobs. Know which is yours.

By Christiaan De Leeuw · Stonehaven Lending · Updated July 30, 2026

The Short Answer

Four reasons: a maturity is due; value has grown enough to pull equity out; terms are better than your note after prepayment costs; or the structure no longer fits. The math: total cost against total benefit over your actual hold.

Maturity

The refinance you do not get to skip

Most commercial mortgages balloon, so the refinance is scheduled the day you close. Start quarters ahead, not weeks: appraisals, environmental reviews and lender processing take time. Negotiating against a maturity date is the weakest hand.

Cash Out

Harvesting created value

Borrow against value you created and keep the asset. Lenders size cash-out with the same DSCR and LTV discipline as a purchase, often tighter. Ask what leverage keeps the property resilient, not the maximum check.

The Math

Prepayment changes everything

Step-down penalties, yield maintenance, defeasance - any can turn a win into a wash. Compare all-in: penalty plus closing costs against savings over your actual hold. Payback in year six is worthless if you sell in year three.

Structure

Sometimes the payment is not the point

Releasing a guarantee, fixing a floating rate, extending term, consolidating notes - the win is the shape of the debt. Placement matters most here; lenders treat structure differently.
