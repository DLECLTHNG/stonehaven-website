What happens to HELOC payments when the draw period ends?

HELOC payments can change when the draw period ends. Compare interest-only and repayment examples, check your agreement and prepare for the transition.

When a HELOC draw period ends, new borrowing generally stops and repayment follows the schedule in your agreement. Your payment can increase because you must repay principal, the rate changes, or both. Some agreements can require a large balance payment. Find the actual end date and repayment terms before treating today's payment as a long-term budget.

The CFPB guide to HELOC draw and repayment periods explains the transition and possible payment changes. The examples below are our own calculations with assumed terms, not quotes or predictions. Stonehaven is a mortgage broker; servicing instructions and your contractual payment schedule must come from your existing servicer.

Draw period versus repayment period

During the draw period, the agreement determines how you can borrow and what you must pay. A line of credit is not the same as a guarantee that you can redraw forever. During repayment, the remaining balance must be paid under the contract's schedule. Ask whether that means amortizing monthly payments, a balance due at once or another structure.

Do not assume every HELOC starts with interest-only payments or uses the same number of years. Record your agreement's dates, current balance, rate index and margin, any fixed-rate portion, minimum-payment calculation and maturity date. If an unfamiliar term affects the payment, ask the servicer to explain it in writing.

Worked example: why a payment can rise without a rate increase

Assume a $60,000 drawn balance and an 8% annual rate. For illustration only, suppose the initial payment is interest-only, then the same balance amortizes over 10 years at that unchanged rate. Using monthly calculations, the payment moves from $400 to approximately $727.97 because the second payment includes principal repayment.

The table excludes fees, additional draws and daily interest conventions, and it holds each modeled rate constant. Your actual agreement can calculate payments differently.

Use the HELOC payment planning tool to change one assumption at a time. Start with the actual balance you expect at the transition, not the maximum credit limit. For a paid-down balance, rerun the model; do not assume your payment will match an example using the original amount.

Four questions for the current servicer

  • When does access end? Confirm the last date for additional borrowing and any separate maturity date.
  • What will I owe next? Request the payment calculation and whether principal amortization or a balloon applies.
  • What could change the amount? Identify rate adjustments, fees and any fixed-rate conversion provisions.
  • What options exist under this agreement? Ask what is actually available, its cost and any qualification requirements.

Keep the servicer's answer and the agreement together. A generic product advertisement cannot override your existing contract. If you expect difficulty making payments, contact the servicer before the transition rather than waiting for a missed payment. This guide cannot change the servicing arrangement or promise an extension.

Separate refinancing from managing the existing account

A new loan is a separate underwriting decision. Do not plan around refinancing until the application, valuation and closing requirements have been satisfied. Compare costs, the new term, the amount of debt and the projected payment with continuing under the existing agreement.

The existing HELOC refinance guide explains how to prepare that comparison. If replacing the first mortgage is also under consideration, read the HELOC versus cash-out refinance comparison. A lower initial payment can still leave more debt or a longer repayment period.

Questions about HELOC repayment

Does the payment always increase when the draw period ends?

Not in every agreement. It depends on balance, rate and repayment structure. An interest-only payment changing to amortization can rise even with an unchanged rate, as the example shows.

Is a fixed-rate conversion the same as refinancing?

No. A conversion, if available, follows the existing line's provisions. Refinancing involves a new loan. Ask for each option's eligibility, cost and effect on access to the remaining line.

Can I keep making only the old payment?

Follow the payment required under your actual agreement. Paying an old amount does not change the new obligation. Ask the servicer to resolve any uncertainty before the due date.

Prepare for a financing conversation

Bring estimated property value, mortgage and HELOC balances, desired financing amount and the repayment transition date. Use the HELOC inquiry form to request a review. Keep account numbers and private documents out of public fields. A home-secured loan puts the property at risk if you cannot repay it, so assess the later obligation alongside the immediate need for funds.
