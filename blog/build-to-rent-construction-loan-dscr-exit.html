Build-to-rent financing: plan the DSCR exit before construction

Building rentals? Test completion, lease-up, rental income and refinance proceeds before relying on a DSCR loan to repay construction debt.

Key testWill refinance proceeds actually cover the payoff?

A build-to-rent construction loan pays for the development stage; a DSCR refinance may provide the long-term exit for an eligible finished rental. They are separate decisions unless the written financing agreement establishes a specific combined structure. Completion alone does not guarantee a refinance or enough proceeds to repay the construction balance.

Stonehaven acts as a mortgage broker and helps coordinate the construction request with the proposed rental financing. Reviewing both early can reveal a gap before the builder spends money on a plan that depends on an unsupported exit.

Work backward from the completed rental

  • Confirm the finished property type, unit count, ownership entity and intended rental use.
  • Support achievable rent with appropriate market evidence; identify lease and occupancy conditions.
  • Estimate taxes, insurance and any association charges that enter the proposed DSCR payment calculation.
  • Check completion evidence, certificate-of-occupancy requirements and remaining work.
  • Clarify title, ownership seasoning, valuation basis, refinance purpose and prepayment conditions.
  • Compare estimated net proceeds with the projected construction payoff at the actual refinance date.

A 1-4-unit rental, a 5-8-unit building and a unified rental community can require different underwriting. Use our 5-8-unit guide when the unit count moves beyond a typical small-property DSCR route.

A hypothetical DSCR sensitivity check

Assume eligible monthly rent of $4,200 and a proposed full monthly housing payment of $3,300. For this illustration, DSCR is rent divided by that payment, or approximately 1.27. This is one residential investment calculation, not a universal program definition or qualification threshold.

Rental exit sensitivity, rounded to two decimal places
Illustrative changeResult
Base: $4,200 rent ÷ $3,300 payment1.27 DSCR
Rent 10% lower: $3,780 ÷ $3,3001.15 DSCR
Payment $300 higher: $4,200 ÷ $3,6001.17 DSCR
Both changes: $3,780 ÷ $3,6001.05 DSCR

A larger proposed payment can result from a different loan amount, financing terms or property expenses. Ask what payment and eligible rent the actual program uses. Commercial multifamily underwriting may instead use net operating income and annual debt service, so do not transplant this example into every property type.

Then test whether the refinance pays off construction

Suppose the projected construction payoff is $900,000. If an illustrative refinance produces an $880,000 gross loan and $20,000 is used for closing costs and required escrows, only $860,000 remains for payoff. The cash gap is $40,000. These figures are assumptions, not quoted proceeds. The existence of equity or an acceptable coverage ratio does not eliminate that gap.

Model a later completion date and slower lease-up as well. Additional interest, insurance and operating expenses can increase the cash needed while waiting for the exit. An extension or bridge loan is a separate financing decision, not an automatic backup.

When should a builder look at HUD instead?

For an eligible apartment development or cohesive rental community intended for a long hold, a HUD construction or build-to-rent route may deserve review at the planning stage. It has a separate process and eligibility requirements. Compare HUD 221(d)(4) and HUD build-to-rent financing. Do not assume a scattered collection of rental houses qualifies as one HUD project.

Can I switch from selling to renting after construction?

Discuss the alternative early. A house designed for a sale price may not generate enough rent for the intended refinance. Review the business plan, financing documents and actual rental economics before changing course. The construction loan’s maturity continues to matter while you arrange the next loan.

Start with one construction and rental summary

Provide the location, unit count, cost, requested construction loan, projected finished value, expected rent, development stage and timeline. Stonehaven can help compare the two stages and identify missing facts. Start a construction financing review or use the DSCR calculator as an educational starting point.
