Self-Employed Jumbo Mortgages: Prepare Your Income

Buying a luxury home as a business owner? Organize income, business cash, down payment and documentation before comparing jumbo mortgage options.

A self-employed jumbo mortgage review starts by separating business activity from income available to repay a personal home loan. Revenue, profit, owner distributions and account balances describe different things. Presenting them clearly helps identify the documentation route worth reviewing before you commit to a luxury-home purchase.

Stonehaven Lending arranges high-value residential financing in AL, SC, NC, GA, TN and FL as a mortgage brokerage. We can review business-owner and complex-income scenarios. Eligibility, usable income and approval depend on the actual program and underwriting, not the size of the company’s deposits alone.

Build a one-page business and purchase summary

List the businesses you own, your ownership share, how long each has operated and how you receive income. Identify whether your role or business structure has recently changed. Then add the home’s state, price range, requested mortgage, down payment and intended occupancy.

You do not need to put tax returns or statements into the initial public form. Start with a concise summary and use the appropriate document process discussed afterward. Our jumbo mortgage overview explains how this income review fits the broader purchase.

Separate revenue, expenses and personal income

Suppose a business receives $100,000 in a month and pays $70,000 in operating expenses. The $100,000 of incoming money is not automatically $100,000 of personal monthly qualifying income. Ownership, transfers, taxes, recurring expenses and the program’s calculation still matter. This is a simplified accounting illustration, not an underwriting formula.

If you pay yourself a salary and also receive distributions, show both and identify the source. Avoid counting a transfer from a business account to a personal account as new income a second time. Keep loan proceeds, owner contributions and transfers clearly labeled so they are not confused with customer revenue.

Tax-return and bank-statement paths answer different questions

A tax-return review examines the documentation required by the selected program, including how business income is analyzed. Fannie Mae’s self-employed underwriting guidance illustrates income-history and cash-flow review for agency loans. It is not a universal jumbo rulebook.

A bank-statement mortgage may be a documentation route to evaluate for a qualifying self-employed buyer. It does not mean every deposit counts, expenses disappear or credit and property requirements are waived. Ask which statement period, accounts, expense treatment and additional records apply to the actual proposal.

Do not assume either route is automatically cheaper or more suitable. Compare the documented income result, down payment, reserves, payment structure and total financing costs. A route that supports a larger loan may still require more cash or a different payment than you want.

Keep business operating cash separate from purchase cash

Create a funds schedule showing personal accounts, business accounts, expected transfers and cash needed after closing. If you intend to withdraw business funds, explain the business’s upcoming obligations and why the withdrawal does not leave a funding shortage. Do not assume a balance shown today remains available on the closing date.

The down-payment and reserves guide shows how a larger contribution can reduce the mortgage while sharply reducing liquidity. Your business accountant can help assess the operating and tax consequences; mortgage approval does not replace that advice.

Explain variable compensation and recent changes early

If part of the household income comes from bonuses, commissions, partnership payments or equity awards, identify it separately from base salary. Explain what is recurring, what has already been received and what remains contingent. A future award or expected contract is not the same as cash already available.

For a relocation, business sale or new ownership structure, describe the change before requesting a firm financing timetable. Keep the mortgage reviewer informed if the facts change during the application. A quote based on a prior income arrangement may need a new review.

Put the document requests in a workable order

  • First conversation: purchase goal, state, income types, business ownership and timing.
  • Documentation review: the specific personal and business records requested through the appropriate process.
  • Scenario comparison: usable income, proposed loan, down payment, reserves and payment structure.
  • Property and closing review: valuation, insurance, contract dates and remaining conditions.

When written offers are available, use the CFPB’s offer-comparison guidance to compare matching loan scenarios. A preliminary discussion is not a commitment to lend.

Questions from business-owner buyers

Does self-employment prevent a jumbo mortgage?

It does not by itself answer the eligibility question. The actual income documentation, credit, assets, property and requested amount must be reviewed. Share the business facts rather than assuming salary is the only acceptable income type.

Can I qualify using gross deposits alone?

Do not assume that. Transfers, nonbusiness deposits, expenses and the program’s rules affect the analysis. A statement balance or gross revenue total is not a preapproval.

Can I buy a second home with business-owner income?

You can submit that scenario for review. Disclose the intended personal and rental use accurately. Occupancy and income are separate parts of the decision, and the proposed property must fit the program.
