Planning a separate home for an adult child with a disability

Separate the person’s housing preferences, the mortgage review, ownership questions, and benefits advice when planning a family-supported home.

Stonehaven Lending · 2026-09-12

Guide overview
FocusDecisions before choosing a home
ReviewSubject to lender requirements

A separate home is one possible housing arrangement, not a definition of independence or a substitute for chosen support. Start with the adult child’s preferences and the practical reasons for considering a move. Keep the financing discussion alongside that planning rather than allowing a loan label to decide the living arrangement.

Keep the person’s choices central

Discuss location, privacy, transportation, daily routines, and the people the adult child wants involved. Consider different types of housing and support without assuming one answer fits every family. A property can look suitable in a listing while raising practical questions that require a visit or specialist advice.

Understand the limited mortgage provision

Fannie Mae’s occupancy guidance addresses parents or legal guardians providing housing for an adult child with a disability who cannot work or has insufficient income to qualify independently. The lender determines whether the circumstances fit and completes ordinary underwriting. Disability alone does not establish mortgage approval.

Separate ownership and benefits questions

Before deciding who should own the property or pay particular expenses, seek advice appropriate to the person’s actual benefits and legal arrangements. For SSI specifically, Social Security explains how living arrangements and help with shelter can affect benefits. Do not assume mortgage occupancy treatment answers that separate question or that one benefit program’s rules apply to another.

Ask a qualified benefits adviser or attorney about the proposed arrangement before signing ownership documents. The broker can explain a lender’s financing requirements, but the mortgage review does not replace advice about benefits, trusts, legal authority, or long-term planning.

Plan for the ordinary work of keeping a home

Who will arrange repairs, receive service providers, manage bills, and respond if the usual support person is unavailable? Discuss what the adult child wants to do personally and what assistance they choose. Write down unresolved responsibilities rather than treating closing day as the end of the plan.

An illustrative family discussion

A parent and adult child compare staying in the current arrangement with moving to a nearby home. They separately review the person’s preferences, practical support, legal and benefits questions, and financing possibilities. This is a hypothetical planning example, not a customer story or a recommendation about anyone’s care.

What belongs in the first inquiry?

Start with the property state, general purchase plans, and the financing question. Do not send diagnoses, medical records, Social Security numbers, or benefits documents through the public form. Ask how any required information can be handled through an appropriate secure process if you choose to proceed.
