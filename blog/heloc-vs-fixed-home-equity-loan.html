HELOC or fixed home equity loan: compare the payment structure

Compare a HELOC with a fixed home equity loan: access to funds, payment changes, fees and the balance remaining when you plan to repay.

A HELOC provides a credit line you may draw from during an agreed period. A fixed home equity loan advances a lump sum with a fixed interest rate. The useful comparison is how each handles your spending schedule, payment budget and payoff date. Neither is automatically cheaper. Both are secured by your home.

Match the funding to the spending schedule

Write down the invoices you know, the amounts that are still estimates and when each becomes due. If you need $80,000 immediately for a defined expense, compare both offers using $80,000 received on the same date. If you need $50,000 now and may need another $30,000 later, ask what the line requires you to draw initially and what conditions apply to later access.

Do not treat unused credit as cash already in your bank. Later access can be restricted under the agreement. Keep a separate plan for a bill that must be paid even if a future draw is unavailable. A lump-sum loan can leave you paying for money before the corresponding invoice is due; the actual offers determine whether that tradeoff is worthwhile.

Compare the same amount over the same holding period. The following is an illustrative accounting exercise, not two available loan offers. The payment totals and balances are assumed, not calculated from a quoted rate.

What happens when the HELOC draw period ends?

Check the repayment schedule before signing. The next stage may require principal repayment or a balloon balance, changing the payment materially. A fixed-rate conversion within a HELOC applies to the balance and terms specified in that agreement; it is not the same product as a separate fixed home equity loan. The CFPB HELOC explanation covers these distinctions.

Which fees belong in the comparison?

Request written charges for opening, maintaining, converting and closing the account. Depending on the agreement, these can include appraisal or title costs, annual or inactivity charges, fixed-rate conversion fees and early cancellation charges. Verify which fees apply to your expected payoff date. The CFPB fee list is a useful starting checklist, not a fee schedule for Stonehaven.

Prepare a review with Stonehaven

Stonehaven is a mortgage broker. Our HELOC inquiry minimums are a $50,000 requested line and a 640 credit score; they are not approval criteria by themselves. Share estimated home value, mortgage balance, requested amount and property state through the HELOC review form. Availability and terms depend on underwriting.

If replacing your existing first mortgage is also an option, compare HELOC versus cash-out refinancing separately. That changes a different part of your financing. Keep your existing mortgage payment in the budget when it remains in place. Because your home secures the borrowing, missed repayment can put it at risk. Do not send SSNs, account numbers or private documents through the inquiry form.
