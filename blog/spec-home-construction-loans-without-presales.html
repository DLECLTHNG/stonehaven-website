A spec home construction loan finances a home intended for sale before a buyer is under contract. Some programs can consider that structure, but the absence of a presale makes the proposed selling price, market demand, builder experience and available cash especially important. A finished-lot purchase, vertical build and eventual sale need to work as one financing plan.

Stonehaven acts as a mortgage broker for business-purpose construction projects. We help builders organize the request and compare the financing structure. This guide concerns investment builds, not a consumer borrowing to construct a personal residence.

What changes when there is no presale?

A comparable sale supports a value discussion; it does not reserve a buyer. Prepare a realistic selling period, a carrying-cost allowance and a response if the house sells below the original estimate. Ask whether a proposal imposes a presale requirement, a limit on unsold homes or conditions before starting another build.

The first review: six facts that make the project understandable

  • Site control: owned, under contract or still being negotiated. Show acquisition cost and any payoff separately.
  • Readiness: zoning, utility access, plans and permit status. Identify unfinished site work rather than calling the lot ready.
  • Full budget: land, demolition if needed, hard costs, soft costs, financing allowances and contingency.
  • Sale case: proposed finished specification, comparable sales and expected marketing period.
  • Execution: the sponsor’s relevant completions, contractor role and current project workload.
  • Cash: contribution at closing plus the money available between construction draws.

A sale-price sensitivity example

This is hypothetical planning arithmetic, not a Stonehaven closing or an offered loan. Assume total project cost of $1,600,000, including the stated financing allowances but excluding selling costs; a $1,360,000 loan; and a projected sale at $2,100,000. The cost-minus-loan gap is $240,000. It is not a final cash-to-close quote because accepted land equity, advances and excluded costs still need review.

Can land equity cover the down payment?

It can support a request when accepted under the proposed program, but land value is not cash available for deposits or payroll. Keep the purchase basis, supported current value and mortgage payoff separate. Read our owned-property construction guide before treating appreciation as an available contribution.

Can a first-time spec builder qualify?

That requires a specific review. Hiring an experienced contractor does not automatically satisfy a sponsor-experience requirement. Describe who completed prior projects, in what role, who manages this build and who covers a shortfall. We review the sponsor and contractor roles alongside the budget, cash position and repayment plan.

Questions to settle before accepting terms

  • Is the lot advance separate from the construction holdback?
  • What must be completed before the first draw, and which costs are reimbursed?
  • Is a buyer contract required at any stage?
  • What happens if construction finishes but the home remains unsold?
  • Are extensions discretionary, and what notice, fees or performance conditions apply?
