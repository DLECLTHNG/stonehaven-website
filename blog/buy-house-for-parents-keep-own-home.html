Can I buy a house for my parents and keep my own home?

Separate the occupancy question from the household budget when buying a home for a parent while keeping your current mortgage.

Stonehaven Lending · 2026-09-12

Guide overview
FocusDecisions before choosing a home
ReviewSubject to lender requirements

Keeping your own home does not automatically end the conversation about buying a separate home for a parent. The useful question is whether the actual arrangement fits an available mortgage and whether you can sustain the combined commitments. Start with those two questions before shopping for a particular house.

First, explain who will live where

Fannie Mae occupancy guidance includes a child buying housing for a parent who cannot work or whose income is inadequate to qualify independently. The lender confirms whether that provision applies. It does not turn every home for a relative into a qualifying primary residence.

Give the broker a plain description: you plan to stay in your current home, and your parent plans to live in the proposed property. Explain any other intended occupants or uses. Accurate occupancy is part of the review, not something to choose after the application has been prepared.

Build a two-home budget

List the proposed mortgage payment, property taxes, insurance, and applicable association charges. Fannie Mae describes the components of the subject property housing expense. Keep your existing housing commitments visible alongside the new proposal and ask the lender how it will evaluate the complete file.

Create a second list for your own planning: maintenance, travel between homes, utilities, and the reserve you want to keep for an unexpected repair. That household budget is not the same as the lender calculation. A loan can deserve a closer budget discussion even when a preliminary calculation looks workable.

Do not build the plan around an informal promise

If a parent or sibling expects to contribute, describe that arrangement before relying on it. Ask whether the lender can use the contribution and what documentation would be required. Separately, decide how the household would manage if the contribution stopped. This is a planning question, not a prediction about anyone’s circumstances.

A hypothetical example

An adult child wants a parent nearby but wants to keep the family’s current home. They compare two candidate properties, including maintenance responsibilities and recurring expenses. Before making an offer, they ask the broker to review the occupancy arrangement and both housing commitments. This is an illustration, not a completed Stonehaven transaction.

What should I prepare for the first conversation?

Prepare the property state, an approximate purchase budget, your current housing obligations, and a target timeline. Do not upload statements or account numbers through a public inquiry. If several relatives will help, read the sibling planning guide. For the move itself, use the parent relocation checklist.
