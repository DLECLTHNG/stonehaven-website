Bridge-to-HUD: Plan the Refinance Before Maturity

Plan a bridge-to-HUD apartment refinance around income, payoff, reserves and timing. Work through a hypothetical proceeds gap before the bridge matures.

Guide overview
PurposeInterim financing with a separately tested exit
Key riskPermanent proceeds may be below the payoff
Starting pointIncome, repairs, maturity and cash plan

A bridge-to-HUD plan uses short-term financing while an eligible property becomes ready for a permanent HUD-insured loan. The bridge may solve an acquisition deadline or fund a transition. It does not guarantee that the eventual refinance will close, cover the entire payoff or produce cash out.

The safest time to test that exit is before signing the bridge loan. Waiting until maturity turns missing documentation, weaker income or a repair issue into an immediate cash problem.

Work backward from the permanent loan

For apartments, start by testing the proposed 223(f) exit against the actual property. Confirm eligibility, repair classification, supportable operating income, loan sizing and the expected timing. A projected appraisal alone is not an exit analysis.

Recently completed properties have a specific path under the MAP Guide. Application can begin after at least one full month at the applicable DSCR, but three consecutive months are required before endorsement, along with the other conditions. One month of leasing does not guarantee one month to refinance.

Calculate the payoff gap before it becomes urgent

Hypothetical proceeds test

Assume the permanent gross loan underwrites to $6.8 million. The bridge payoff at the projected closing is $6.7 million, and transaction costs, reserves and required deposits use $350,000. Net proceeds available for payoff are $6.45 million, leaving a $250,000 gap. These assumed amounts are not a quote or a closed deal.

A higher appraisal does not automatically cure the gap if debt service coverage controls loan sizing. Interest accrued during a delay can enlarge the payoff while weaker collections reduce the replacement loan. Test both changes together.

Build a timeline with decision points

  • Before bridge closing: Identify the permanent program, likely sizing constraints, required repairs and operating evidence.
  • During ownership: Maintain rent rolls, collections, expenses, concessions and repair records that reconcile.
  • Before maturity: Allow time for reports, underwriting, HUD review, firm-commitment conditions and closing.
  • If the schedule slips: Know extension fees, minimum interest, rate changes, liquidity conditions and alternatives before relying on an extension.

An extension option may be conditional. A possible sale or a second refinance also requires its own realistic proceeds and timing analysis.

Confirm costs and responsibilities in writing

Ask which reports may be reusable, when they expire, who orders them, who owns them and whether the permanent process requires different scope. Do not assume every bridge appraisal or environmental report will transfer without further work.

Compare bridge prepayment terms with the earliest realistic HUD closing. A permanent loan can be ready while a minimum-interest or exit-fee provision still changes the economics.

How Stonehaven can help prepare the exit

Share the property, existing loan balance, maturity, recent operations, completed and remaining work, and your target refinance amount. We can help organize the timing and proceeds questions before the next underwriting step.

For apartments, continue with HUD 223(f) financing. For development, review 221(d)(4). Eligible care facilities use a separate Section 232 process, so apartment assumptions should not be transferred to healthcare.
