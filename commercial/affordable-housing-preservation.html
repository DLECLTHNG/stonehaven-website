Financing an affordable apartment property with rent restrictions

Preservation financing pays for the purchase, refinance or repair of existing affordable apartments while keeping the applicable rent and occupancy restrictions in place. Start with the recorded agreement and the dates any rental assistance expires.

Which units are restricted, and by which agreement?

Ask for the recorded use agreement, amendments and a unit count for each restriction. Identify the rent and income limits, the period they cover, renewal options, and approvals required for a sale or new debt. If a project has rental assistance, include the contract term and how much revenue comes from assistance and resident payments.

Keep market-rate units on a separate schedule. A building charging below-market rents may have no recorded restrictions. A building marketed as workforce housing may have enforceable restrictions on only part of the property. Have the owner’s counsel confirm the agreements that run with the land.

Build a rent schedule that matches the documents

Hypothetical property. Assume 12 of 40 apartments can collect $1,200 a month under their agreements, while the other 28 collect $1,500. At full collection, the annual total is $676,800. Modeling all 40 at $1,500 would produce $720,000, overstating annual revenue by $43,200 before vacancy, concessions or expenses.

Use the rent applicable to each unit after any required utility allowance. Show actual collections alongside permitted rents. If a subsidy pays part of the contract rent, count the resident payment and subsidy once each, avoiding double-counting total contract rent.

Match the financing review to the property’s obligations

Fannie Mae’s Affordable Housing Preservation term sheet addresses stabilized properties with documented affordability, including certain properties with expiring low-income housing tax credits or project-based assistance. Freddie Mac publishes separate targeted affordable housing term sheets. Confirm the eligible program and term for the actual agreements. Fannie Mae preservation criteria · Freddie Mac targeted affordable housing.

A preservation refinance can require money for roofs, mechanical systems or occupied-unit repairs. Put the repair reserve, subordinate debt and resident relocation costs, if applicable, on the same sources-and-uses schedule. For substantial rehabilitation or new construction, compare the HUD construction and substantial rehabilitation path early.

If the first mortgage has years left and the repairs are modest, compare a supplemental loan with replacing the first mortgage. Check whether both the first loan and the affordability agreement permit additional debt.

What should the first inquiry include?

Send the state, total and restricted units, acquisition price or payoff, financing requested, general type of restriction, and the next contract or debt expiry. Stonehaven can organize a financing review around those dates. Keep resident names, household certifications and financial records off the inquiry form. The initial comparison only needs property-level totals.
