What does HUD Section 232 cover?

Section 232 mortgage insurance supports eligible residential healthcare facilities, including nursing homes, assisted living and board-and-care facilities. It can support eligible purchase, refinancing, new construction and substantial rehabilitation transactions. HUD’s Office of Residential Care Facilities administers the program using the LEAN process.

Healthcare financing requires a review of both the real estate and the operating business. An apartment rent roll is not a substitute for the facility’s licensing, staffing, financial and regulatory picture.

Identify the facility before selecting the program

  • Nursing facilities: Eligible licensed facilities generally require at least 20 beds under the handbook.
  • Assisted living: Licensing, services and the handbook’s definition matter, including the general 20-bed minimum.
  • Board and care: The handbook uses a minimum of 20 accommodations, with its own operating definition.
  • Independent living: A standalone independent-living property is not automatically a Section 232 facility. Incidental independent-living units within an eligible project have specific limits and review requirements.
  • Other uses: Hospitals and ordinary medical offices are not automatically Section 232 properties. Hospitals have a separate Section 242 program.

Check the licensed use and actual services rather than relying on a property listing labeled “senior housing.” HUD’s facility eligibility chapter provides the controlling definitions.

Separate the real estate owner and operator

The borrower, operator, lease arrangements and management structure all affect review. Relevant information includes ownership, operating experience, licenses, occupancy, payer mix, operating results, inspections and capital needs. Related-party arrangements and management agreements should be identified early.

The initial review should use business-level summaries. Do not send patient names, records, diagnoses, benefits information or other personal health information through a public inquiry form.

Do not import apartment terms into healthcare

The 2025 multifamily ratio letter expressly excludes Section 232, and the multifamily MIP reduction is not a universal healthcare premium. The applicable healthcare loan-sizing tests, coverage requirements, insurance and term must be established for the transaction.

Similarly, the 2025 update to the healthcare substantial rehabilitation definition belongs to the Section 232 rules. Do not apply an apartment repair threshold to a care-facility renovation. Read the healthcare rehabilitation update.

A fixed-rate, long-term structure may suit a stabilized operating facility. If occupancy, operations or licensing are changing, a bridge-to-HUD proposal needs an independently underwritten exit and enough time for stabilization, review and closing.

Prepare a healthcare financing summary

  • Facility type, city and state, licensed beds or accommodations and current occupancy.
  • Purchase price or estimated value, existing debt, requested financing and use of funds.
  • Owner and operator structure, operating history and relevant experience.
  • Recent financial trends, payer mix and any material licensing or inspection issues.
  • Renovation scope, capital needs, timing and the planned long-term operating model.

We can help organize the financing conversation around these business facts and identify the next document step. For ordinary rental apartments, use the 223(f) apartment guide instead.

What affects the timeline?

The quality of the operating file, regulatory issues, transaction complexity and the HUD review queue all matter. HUD publishes healthcare underwriting queue information, but a queue position is not a closing-date guarantee.

Resolve a maturity or acquisition deadline before committing to a permanent-loan schedule. Bridge costs, extension conditions and the consequences of a delayed takeout should be clear in the comparison.
