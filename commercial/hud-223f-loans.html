What does HUD 223(f) finance?

Section 223(f) supports the purchase or refinancing of eligible existing multifamily rental properties with at least five units. It can accommodate qualifying repairs, but it is not the route for work that meets HUD’s substantial rehabilitation definition. The property’s income, condition and operating history must support permanent financing.

Terms can extend to 35 years, limited by 75% of remaining economic life. A fixed rate and long amortization may suit a long-term hold, but financing costs, MIP, reserves and exit restrictions still belong in the comparison.

How much can an apartment property borrow?

Current market-rate guidance allows up to 87% applicable LTV with a 1.15 DSCR, subject to all other limits. Eligible affordable categories can use different ratios. The approved amount is the lowest result of the applicable mortgage criteria, including value, debt service, transaction costs and statutory per-unit limits. HUD’s current ratio letter.

A value ceiling is not a cash-out quote

The 2025 ratio increase expressly left Criterion 10 cash-out rules unchanged. The MAP Guide describes that criterion as the greater of 80% of value or the cost to refinance, subject to other mortgage limits. It does not permit automatic 87% cash-out. Repair escrows and release conditions may also hold back cash.

Before estimating proceeds, separate existing payoff, transaction costs, repairs, deposits, required reserves and distributable cash. Compare the net result with your objective, not just the gross loan amount.

Does the building have to be three years old?

A blanket three-year waiting rule is outdated. The MAP Guide allows certain recently completed or substantially rehabilitated properties to apply after achieving the applicable program DSCR for at least one full month. It requires three consecutive months of the required coverage before endorsement, plus the specific operating documentation and safeguards for that category.

That is not an automatic one-month closing. Rent concessions, achieved collections, normalized expenses and the remaining lease-up plan need review. Newly completed build-to-rent communities also have the separate 2026 BTR eligibility rules.

Repairs can change the financing route

Prepare a scope that separates life-safety items, accessibility work, ordinary repairs and major building-system work. Do not classify a property as light rehabilitation just because the purchase contract uses that phrase.

HUD’s substantial rehabilitation analysis considers more than a single dollar amount. The 2026 base per-unit threshold is $20,406 before applicable adjustments and the other tests in the MAP Guide. It is not a universal $20,406 repair allowance. Have the actual scope classified before budgeting a 223(f) closing. If it is substantial rehabilitation, compare 221(d)(4).

Prepare the acquisition or refinance

  • Provide the current rent roll, lease concessions, trailing operating statements and occupancy history.
  • Reconcile taxes, insurance, utilities, management and replacement needs against actual records.
  • Identify purchase terms or debt payoff, prepayment obligations, ownership structure and any related-party sale.
  • Flag repairs, environmental conditions and affordability restrictions before reports are ordered.
  • Show the timing of deposits and any equity contribution separately from loan proceeds.

For a short acquisition deadline, compare a bridge-to-HUD plan with the costs and risks of waiting for permanent debt. A bridge loan does not guarantee the later HUD refinance.
