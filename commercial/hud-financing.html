What is a HUD commercial real estate loan?

HUD multifamily financing uses FHA mortgage insurance to support loans made by approved lenders for eligible rental housing. It can provide long-term, fixed-rate financing for existing apartments, new rental construction and substantial rehabilitation. Eligible healthcare facilities use a separate Section 232 process. HUD insurance is not a grant, a rental subsidy or an automatic approval.

Stonehaven helps owners and developers compare the financing structure, prepare the project and understand the next step. The property, its income, the development team and the intended hold period determine whether a HUD route deserves a closer look.

Choose the route that matches the property

Existing apartments: 223(f)

Purchase or refinance an eligible operating apartment property with repairs below the substantial rehabilitation threshold.

Construction: 221(d)(4)

Plan new apartments or substantial rehabilitation with construction financing and long-term permanent debt.

Build-to-rent communities

Review the new rules for qualifying detached rental homes, duplexes and triplexes on a cohesive site.

Healthcare: Section 232

Evaluate eligible nursing, assisted living and board-and-care facilities through the ORCF/LEAN process.

Start with fit, then size the loan

  • Property: Apartment programs generally start at five rental units. That is an eligibility floor, not a promise that a five-unit loan is economical.
  • Business plan: Separate an operating-property refinance from construction, major rehabilitation and a sale of individual homes.
  • Income: Use supportable rents and normalized operating expenses. A large appraisal cannot replace sufficient debt service coverage.
  • Sponsor: Experience, liquidity, ownership structure and management matter alongside the building.
  • Timing: Reports, underwriting, HUD review and closing conditions need room in the schedule. An urgent maturity may require a separately underwritten bridge plan.

Standalone office, retail, industrial buildings and a collection of unrelated one-to-four-unit rentals do not become eligible apartment projects simply because they are commercial investments. Compare other commercial financing or DSCR rental financing when the property does not fit.

Current terms worth understanding

For covered market-rate 223(f) and 221(d)(4) transactions, current HUD guidance permits up to 87% applicable value or eligible cost with a 1.15 debt service coverage ratio. Qualifying affordable categories may reach 90% with 1.11 coverage. Eligible middle-income housing has a separate 221(d)(4) route. These are ceilings and tests, not quoted proceeds. Read HUD’s ratio update.

The lowest applicable sizing limit controls. Cash-out restrictions, per-unit statutory limits, expenses, reserves and lender underwriting can reduce available proceeds. The 87% headline does not mean an owner can automatically extract 87% of appraised value.

Covered multifamily loans have a 0.25% annual mortgage insurance premium under the notice applying to applications submitted or amended from October 1, 2025, before initial endorsement. MIP is separate from interest and upfront funding requirements. It is not a blanket reduction for already-endorsed loans or Section 232 healthcare loans. Read the final MIP notice.

A useful first review package

  • Property location, unit count, current ownership and whether the request is a purchase, refinance or development.
  • Requested loan, estimated value or development budget, existing debt and the real closing deadline.
  • For an operating property: current rent roll, recent operating results, occupancy, concessions and planned repairs.
  • For development: site control, zoning, plans, construction budget, contractor and development experience.
  • Known environmental, title, insurance, affordability or operating issues. Identify them early rather than ordering reports on incomplete assumptions.

Questions to resolve before choosing HUD

Is HUD only for subsidized housing?

No. Eligible market-rate apartments can qualify. Affordable and middle-income classifications have their own criteria; ordinary affordable rents do not automatically establish a special category.

Is the financing nonrecourse?

HUD-insured multifamily structures generally offer nonrecourse debt, subject to loan-document exceptions and applicable construction or completion obligations. Nonrecourse does not mean the owner has no obligations.

What does it cost and how long does it take?

Request a property-specific schedule and a written breakdown of reports, application costs, financing charges, insurance and reserves. Do not rely on a universal rate, closing-time promise or minimum loan amount.
