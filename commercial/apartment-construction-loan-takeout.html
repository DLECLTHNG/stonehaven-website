Refinancing an apartment construction loan during lease-up

An apartment construction loan takeout repays the construction lender, usually with longer-term debt. Compare the maturity date with the month occupied units are expected to generate enough collected rent to support the new loan.

Track delivered units, move-ins and collections separately

A certificate of occupancy, a signed lease, an occupied apartment and a first rent payment record different events. Build a monthly schedule for all four. Include concessions, delinquency and remaining construction retainage. A lease signed in June with a July move-in and one free month may first produce rent in August.

For financing discussions, show the construction balance and expected final draws, the maturity and extension notice date, interest reserve balance, and operating cash needed until collections cover expenses. Ask the servicer for the notice and documents required to request an extension.

Compare three ways to reach permanent financing

  • Extend the construction loan. Identify the fee, remaining draws, rate protection and occupancy conditions. Work backward from the notice deadline.
  • Use a lease-up bridge. Compare the new closing costs and interest reserve with the additional time it buys. Stress-test the payoff under slower leasing.
  • Review a near-stabilization loan. Fannie Mae publishes a specific permanent financing option for eligible newly built or recently renovated communities. Its term sheet lists a $10 million minimum loan and at least 75% physical occupancy at rate lock, with certificates of occupancy for all residential units at commitment. Sponsor experience, market and achievable coverage also enter its pre-review. Fannie Mae near-stabilization term sheet.

If collections will fall short at maturity, compare bridge and agency debt using the same rent roll before pricing an extension.

How much time does slower leasing cost?

Hypothetical 80-unit building. With 48 occupied apartments today, the sponsor’s own 90% target is 72 occupied units. At six net move-ins a month, it takes four months to add the 24 units. At three per month, it takes eight. Net move-ins already subtract move-outs. This target is for the example.

If the construction loan matures in five months, the slower case leaves a three-month gap before the target, plus time to document collections and close the replacement loan. At an assumed monthly cash shortfall of $30,000, those three months alone require $90,000. Add interest and operating costs only once when building your cash forecast.

Size the payoff before you start the next project

Deduct the construction payoff, remaining bills, new escrows and closing costs from permanent loan proceeds. Record the equity required if that amount is negative. For a development still in design, compare the HUD 221(d)(4) construction route and project-specific construction financing while the draw schedule is still flexible.
