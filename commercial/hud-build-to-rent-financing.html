Can HUD finance a build-to-rent community?

Yes, qualifying communities of detached rental homes, duplexes and triplexes can now be considered under issued Mortgagee Letter 2026-12. The guidance opens 221(d)(4) for new construction and 223(f) for acquisition or refinancing of recently developed BTR communities with certificates of occupancy less than three years old at application.

This is a specific rental-community pathway. It does not turn scattered houses, a for-sale subdivision or every older single-family rental portfolio into a HUD multifamily property. Read the issued BTR letter.

The site and ownership tests come first

  • At least five dwelling units in a single cohesive, contiguous rental development.
  • Multiple contiguous parcels may be acceptable, but privately owned parcels interspersed within the development are not.
  • No sale of individual parcels or buildings and no partial releases of collateral during the insured loan term.
  • Professional management with appropriate local staffing and a team experienced with the project type and scale.
  • Any homeowners association must be disclosed and must not prevent the borrower from controlling and operating the project as required.

For a builder, the exit decision belongs at the start. A project intended to sell homes one by one needs a different financing structure. Read the BTR eligibility article for a practical site-review example.

Underwrite a rental community, not a home-sale plan

The market study needs suitable rental comparisons, realistic absorption and the operating costs of detached homes or smaller buildings. Roofs, exterior maintenance, landscaping and amenities can produce different replacement and operating needs from a conventional apartment block.

The letter sets market-rate underwriting vacancy at the greater of 7% or the comparable-property level. Affordable projects have a 5% minimum, and small communities may warrant higher assumptions. Do not underwrite every house as continuously occupied just because the initial leasing interest is strong.

For new construction, HUD may consider absorption of up to 24 months. The initial operating-deficit reserve is sized to the risk, with a minimum of the greater of 3% of the loan or four months of debt service. Those minimums do not cap the reserve if projected losses require more.

Construction and refinance are different entries

Before construction

Use the 221(d)(4) review to connect site control, plans, costs, contractor experience, lease-up and permanent debt. Confirm environmental and design requirements before locking a budget.

After development

The new BTR 223(f) route is for recently developed communities within the letter’s certificate-of-occupancy window. Operating performance and the underlying 223(f) requirements still apply. Recent completion alone is insufficient.

Factory-built or modular components can raise additional approval and construction-draw questions. Review them at concept stage. The letter also distinguishes dwelling configurations from common-area accessibility obligations; do not treat it as an exemption from all accessibility requirements.

A BTR summary Stonehaven can review

  • City and state, acreage, parcel map and whether the site is contiguous.
  • Number of homes, duplexes or triplexes and the intended rental ownership structure.
  • Total development cost or purchase price, existing debt and requested financing.
  • Zoning, approvals, construction status and certificate-of-occupancy dates if completed.
  • Expected rents, achieved leases, concessions, operating budget and replacement assumptions.
  • Sponsor, contractor and manager experience, plus any HOA or individual-sale plans.
