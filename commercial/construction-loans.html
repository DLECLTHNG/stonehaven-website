The short answer

Ground-up construction loans for builders

A ground-up construction loan finances a new property from the land stage through completion, with construction funds generally released in stages. Stonehaven acts as a mortgage broker for builders and investors. Qualifying projects may be considered for up to 100% of eligible costs, subject to budget, equity, completed value, experience and underwriting. Financing the budget does not mean zero cash required.

Capital for the way you build

Buy, demolish and rebuild

Show the purchase price, demolition cost and new build budget separately. We review the acquisition and construction phases together, including the time needed to obtain permits.

Build on land you own

Land equity may strengthen the structure. Start with your ownership basis, estimated land value and current payoff. Existing debt must be accounted for before equity can support the new loan.

Build to sell or hold

A spec sale and a rental refinance need different exit assumptions. Include comparable sales for a sale exit, or supported rent and expense estimates for a rental plan.

Spec homes without a presale

Prepare comparable sales, a marketing timeline and carrying costs. A project without a contracted buyer needs its own financing review.

Multiple homes and phased starts

Multifamily development

100% LTC and 100% of construction costs are different

Ask what the lender counts as cost before comparing two proposals. A large headline percentage can describe a narrower budget than you expect.

A $3.15M request would equal 90% of the assumed cost and about 65.6% of the assumed completed value. The arithmetic gap is $350,000 before any excluded costs or changes to the lender’s basis.

A $3.5M request would equal 100% of that assumed cost, but only about 72.9% of completed value. Those calculations describe the request, not a lender’s available limits.

If the lender excludes an allowance, advances draws after completed work, or requires additional reserves, the builder can still need cash. A 100% eligible-cost structure must be checked line by line.

How much cash do I need if I already own the land?

Owned land can support the equity contribution, but it does not pay an invoice. Separate the funding gap for the whole project from cash needed at closing and cash needed between draws. This hypothetical example assumes free-and-clear land, no cash out and no other financing.

$400,000
Remaining costs, including modeled fees and reserves
$1,600,000
Total modeled cost basis
$2,000,000
Assumed construction loan commitment
$1,500,000

75% LTC
Remaining costs less new loan commitment
$100,000 funding gap

The $400,000 land contribution is already part of the total cost basis. Do not subtract it again from the $1.6 million still to be paid. With $1.5 million of new financing, the remaining project has a $100,000 funding gap on these assumptions.

A $1.6 million commitment would cover 100% of the modeled remaining costs but equal 80% of the $2 million total cost basis. That arithmetic does not establish an available program or remove requirements for working cash.

If the land has debt, add its payoff to the uses of the new financing and ask how much land equity is recognized. An accepted value, an equity credit and cash released to the borrower are different figures.

1. Cash at closing

Ask for the actual closing advance and a sources-and-uses statement showing payoff, fees, reserves and your contribution. Funds held for later construction cannot pay a closing invoice.

2. Cash between draws

Map contractor invoices against expected reimbursement dates. An $80,000 invoice due before its draw can create an $80,000 temporary cash need. This may overlap with the project contribution, so model timing rather than blindly adding both amounts.

3. Cash if the schedule changes

Run a slower completion and sale scenario. Identify which reserve covers interest, which contingency covers extra work, and what remains your responsibility if either runs out.

Compare the funding schedule, not just the commitment

At closing

Identify what funds acquisition or payoff, which costs have already been paid, and whether your equity goes in before loan proceeds.

During construction

Confirm inspection requirements, draw documentation, reimbursement timing, retainage and treatment of change orders. Keep a working-cash plan between draws. Ask which milestones release funds and whether deposits require your cash before reimbursement.

At completion

Build time for listing, sale or lease-up into the exit. Check maturity, extension conditions and the payoff calculation before relying on a refinance. Match the proposed maturity to construction plus marketing time; get extension conditions, notice deadlines and any costs in writing.

What to send for an initial construction review

  • Property location, purchase contract or existing ownership and payoff summary.
  • Line-item budget separating acquisition, demolition, hard costs, soft costs, reserves and contingency.
  • Plans, permit status and a realistic construction schedule.
  • Builder or general contractor background and relevant completed projects.
  • Estimated completed value with supporting comparable sales, or a rental operating plan.
  • Requested financing, available equity, intended exit and desired closing date.

Confirm these before relying on a high-leverage quote

Raw land held without a defined build plan, owner-occupied consumer construction and partially completed projects need a different review. This page does not establish their eligibility. Environmental issues, unresolved title, unsupported value or an incomplete budget can also change the financing path.

Questions before you start

Can a ground-up construction loan include the land purchase?

A construction request can include acquisition and the build, subject to the selected program. Show the purchase price, closing advance and construction holdback separately. If the land is not ready to build, identify which approvals or site work must be completed before construction funds can be released.

Can I discuss construction financing before permits are complete?

Yes. Start with the current plans, permit status, budget and expected start date so we can identify what is still needed. Early feedback is not an approval to close or start work. Ask which documents and approvals must be complete for the acquisition, demolition and construction stages.

Is ground-up construction financing the same as a fix-and-flip loan?

No. A ground-up project creates a new building, including a teardown and rebuild. A fix-and-flip project generally renovates an existing building for resale. Describe any demolition, structural work and change of use accurately so the financing review matches the actual scope.

Can I get 100% LTC construction financing?

Potentially, on qualifying projects. We review eligible-cost definitions, land equity, value limits, liquidity and guarantees. Funding 100% of the construction budget is different from funding 100% of total project cost or completed value.

Does land equity replace my cash down payment?

It may help, but the lender must accept the value and ownership basis. A mortgage payoff reduces the unencumbered equity, and separate cash may still be needed for excluded costs and construction cash flow.

How do project cost, loan amount and completed value differ?

Total project cost is the budget to acquire and complete the project. The loan amount is the financing requested, and completed value is the supported estimate of what the finished property will be worth. Submit all three separately so the lender can review cost, value and your required contribution.

How does builder experience affect the review?

An experienced builder can show comparable completions. A first-time sponsor should explain the contractor team, oversight and liquidity. Ask which experience requirements apply; this page does not promise a first-time-builder program.

Is interest charged on funds advanced or the full commitment?

Confirm the calculation basis in the proposal. If interest-only payments are proposed, request a sample draw-by-draw payment schedule, including any minimum-interest or unused-funds charges. Interest-only payments do not reduce principal.

How quickly will I get feedback?

We aim to provide initial deal feedback in under one hour after the inquiry reaches our team. That is an initial response, not a credit decision, term sheet or closing commitment. We follow up by text.

Can I finance a spec home without a buyer under contract?

Some programs consider build-to-sell projects without a presale. Prepare market demand, comparable sales, experience, timing and cash through sale. Eligibility needs a project-specific review.

Can a DSCR loan pay off the construction loan?

It may be an exit for an eligible completed rental, but it requires separate underwriting. Check rent, expenses, occupancy, value and net refinance proceeds against the construction payoff.
