What if the build costs more or the appraisal is lower?

These tests keep the base loan ceiling fixed when hard costs increase. A lower completed value may reduce proceeds before closing. No increase in the loan is assumed. Treat the hard-cost increase as an additional unfunded overrun, without reusing the contingency already in your budget.

How the construction loan calculation works

  1. Eligible cost basis: accepted land basis plus remaining hard costs, soft costs, contingency and financing allowance.
  2. Loan ceilings: cost basis multiplied by your LTC limit, and completed value multiplied by your LTV limit.
  3. Uses still to fund: remaining budget plus the purchase price if buying, or existing land payoff if already owned.
  4. Modeled loan: the lowest of the two ceilings and the uses still to fund. This tool does not model cash-out.
  5. Funding gap: uses still to fund less the modeled loan, plus costs excluded from the eligible budget.

Land already owned is included in the cost basis but is not a new cash purchase. Its existing payoff is a cash use. This distinction prevents counting the land contribution twice. Already-paid design fees, multiple liens, other collateral and complex capital stacks require a separate sources-and-uses review.

Example: owned land and a $1.6 million remaining budget

Assume free-and-clear land has an accepted basis of $400,000. Remaining costs are $1.2 million hard costs, $200,000 soft costs, $100,000 contingency and $100,000 financing allowance. The cost basis is $2 million. At an assumed 75% LTC, the cost ceiling is $1.5 million. A $3 million completed value at an assumed 65% LTV gives a $1.95 million value ceiling.

The model uses the lower $1.5 million loan, leaving $100,000 of the remaining budget unfunded. Add a $200,000 land payoff and the gap becomes $300,000. Buying the same land for $400,000 instead produces a $500,000 gap. These examples exclude any additional costs outside the entered budget.

Questions about your result

Can 100% of the construction budget still be less than 100% LTC?

Yes. With $400,000 of accepted land basis and $1.6 million of remaining costs, a $1.6 million loan is 80% of the $2 million total cost basis. It covers the remaining budget without financing the entire cost basis.

Does this estimate cash due at closing?

No. It estimates the overall funding gap. The closing advance, payoff, fees, equity funding sequence, retainage and reimbursement schedule determine when cash is needed.

Does owning land guarantee that no cash is needed?

No. Confirm the accepted basis, existing debt, costs excluded from financing and cash needed between draws. A property can contribute equity and still require cash during construction.
