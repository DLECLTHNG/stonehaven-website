# Known study and Stonehaven implementation

Research date: September 30, 2026. Review preview only, not a production release. No new articles or program claims are authorized by the reference website. Existing Family publication pause remains in effect.

## What was actually studied

The current [Known homepage](https://known.agency/), its [sitemap](https://known.agency/sitemap.xml), [robots file](https://known.agency/robots.txt), server-rendered HTML, rendered page, visible navigation, proof section, consultation calls to action and linked founder/company search results were reviewed. The sitemap lists just the homepage. The visible site links to the founder, LinkedIn, Byword and Mentions, rather than an internal article library. There is no evidence here of a large public Known content cluster to reproduce.

The page was inspected without submitting an inquiry. The browser's primary CTA click did not expose a form in the inspected session; no claim is made about form completion or their lead routing. Video presentation was observed, but not transcribed or treated as a independently verified case study. Search access to the LinkedIn profile was limited; only retrieved public excerpts were considered.

Known's own ranking, backlinks, conversions, client results and market share were not verified. Its superlative is marketing, not an independent ranking measurement. Do not confuse known.agency with known.is or knownagency.com, which also appeared in search results.

## Findings and practical decisions

| Observed pattern | Useful lesson | Stonehaven application |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A concise service proposition with a repeated, specific assessment offer | Explain what someone gets after inquiring | Clarify what the initial commercial review covers and that follow-up is by text |
| Buyer searches and questions are the starting point of their positioning | Organize pages around a financing decision, not a publishing quota | Map construction, renovation, maturity, land and rental acquisition questions to existing URLs |
| Evidence about the founder, tools and audience supports their pitch | Credibility needs identifiable evidence | Preserve Stonehaven's team identity and disclosures; require approved evidence before adding new closings, testimonials or performance numbers |
| Google, AI discovery and external mentions are treated together | Search visibility needs consistent information on and off the site | Keep conventional SEO, useful guides and attribution connected; retain a separate external-authority backlog |
| Their offer describes a diagnosis, not just a contact button | Reduce uncertainty about the next step | State what to send and what an initial review does and does not mean |
| Their positioning emphasizes selective priorities | Work on valuable decisions rather than multiplying pages | Improve the commercial pillar and existing resource hub; no new keyword variants or duplicate service pages |

These are qualitative observations and implementation hypotheses, not proven causes of Known's search performance. Their proof claims and founder results are not Stonehaven's proof and are not reused.

## Technical patterns not copied

The fetched Known homepage was 320,926 bytes of HTML, with no h1, h2 or h3 tags, no HTML language attribute and no JSON-LD blocks in that response. It did include a canonical, description and Open Graph metadata. Its visible sections use styled text. Those facts do not prove that the page cannot rank, but they are not improvements for Stonehaven to imitate.

Stonehaven already has semantic headings, canonical URLs, language alternates, breadcrumbs, stable organization references, server-rendered article text, sitemaps and relevant structured data. Preserve them. Preserve the sized brand assets and avoid importing heavy video, animated text, duplicate responsive text or logo marquees simply because the reference uses them.

Google's [AI features documentation](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features) explains that eligibility relies on ordinary Search fundamentals, with no special AI markup requirement. Its [generative AI optimization guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide) emphasizes useful original content and cautions against supposed AI shortcuts. Existing llms files are retained as a convenience, not presented as a Google ranking mechanism. Discovery excerpts update from the visible content normally.

## What changed in this review

1. English and Spanish commercial pillars now offer six plain-HTML paths near the top: build or rebuild, renovate and sell, refinance or reposition, develop land, buy apartments, and finance a rental. The concise definition stays before these paths. Each link includes an explanation of the decision and points to an existing service page.
2. The redundant three-link program block at the bottom of those pillars is removed. Its destinations remain in the new cards and other contextual links.
3. The commercial resource hub is organized around six real questions: borrowing capacity, bridge versus permanent, maturity, land equity, cash needed with high LTC, and lot-release mechanics. Calculator and methodology links explain the difference between an estimate and a credit decision.
4. The resource title and visible heading explicitly identify commercial real estate financing guides. The obsolete 48-hour card is removed. The review CTA explains text follow-up and distinguishes initial feedback from approval or a term sheet.
5. The commercial pillar's primary CTA goes directly to its existing form. Review expectations are placed before the form without changing field names, values, required fields, consent, routing, CRM integration or tracking.
6. Longer commercial service pages in both languages receive an expandable table of contents using their actual h2 headings. The navigation works without new JavaScript. Existing heading IDs are preserved and new IDs are stable on repeated generation.
7. Overbroad wording about universal geographic availability, salary being irrelevant and automatic term sheets is narrowed on the commercial pillar. No rates, leverage thresholds, minimums or eligibility rules are newly invented.

Maintained source: `scripts/cre_search_journeys.py`, called by the existing final generator. Styling is isolated in `cre-journeys.css`; unrelated shells do not inherit the new stylesheet. The existing static commercial pillar and resource hub own their editorial text.

## What the measurement shows now

`before.json` and `after.json` count distinct indexable pages linking from their main content, excluding navigation/footer links and self-links. The sample counts are unchanged because the priority destinations already had links. This change improves placement, context and navigation, not the total number of referring pages. It would be misleading to claim an increase in authority from these edits alone.

All 381 sitemap URLs are retained. No page is removed or newly noindexed. Existing service pages own the search intents in `search-intent-map.csv`; the examples are proposed buyer queries and AI questions, not measured search volumes or rank positions. Priorities reflect business relevance and readiness, not traffic data.

After a separately approved production release, annotate the release date in the private SEO record. At the already scheduled review, compare equal completed windows using the existing Commercial and DSCR filters. Keep those results distinct from Residential and SBA. Use Search Console impressions, clicks, CTR and indexing, plus the available AI reports and actual qualified CRM inquiries. Explain reporting lag and do not attribute a result to this release without considering other changes.

No GSC/Bing access or metric export was needed for this public-reference study. No mass indexing, account changes or new automation was performed. Existing first-touch attribution, inquiry IDs and successful-submission conversions are preserved.

## Work that cannot honestly be manufactured in a code change

- **Transaction evidence:** obtain approved, anonymized facts for closed construction, renovation and commercial refinance examples. Record cost, requested versus funded amount, land equity, limitations and date. Do not relabel illustrative scenarios as completed transactions.
- **Expert contribution:** ask Chris for the practical financing issue, tradeoff and lesson behind each example. Do not attribute a newly generated opinion or review to him without his review.
- **External authority:** prepare a useful article or project-financing checklist for an actual architect, builder or commercial real estate publication with a relevant audience. Owner approval is needed before outreach, account posting or submission. No purchased links, manufactured reviews or hidden advertorials.
- **Profile consistency:** use verified company/founder profiles and correct organization identity. Do not invent LinkedIn or business-profile URLs to fill a schema field.
- **Evidence-driven expansion:** add another page only when a real search/inquiry pattern exposes a distinct unresolved question. Check the existing owner URL before commissioning another construction, bridge or DSCR article.

## Validation

- All 15 generators completed in CI order; all 181 tests, four linters and SEO checks passed.
- 29 public HTML pages changed: the bilingual commercial pillars, 26 bilingual commercial subpages, and the existing English resource hub.
- Browser checks covered all 29 changed pages at 390 and 1440 pixels, 58 cases. No horizontal overflow, missing h1, multiple h1s or broken contents links were found.
- The guide contents opened using the keyboard. Desktop English and mobile Spanish cards were visually inspected; screenshots and structured results are retained beside this report.
- Form markup, tracking scripts and footer disclosures on the changed pages were compared with the published baseline and remained identical. No real or synthetic lead was submitted.
- All existing sitemap contents are unchanged. No new JavaScript or third-party service is introduced. The new CSS is limited to the changed commercial pages.
- No new Family, FHA, VA, HELOC-persona or first-time-buyer content was written. No website or account publication occurs until approval of the review preview.
