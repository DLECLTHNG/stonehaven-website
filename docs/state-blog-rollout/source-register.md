| North Carolina | Choosing a family home across county lines | [North Carolina Department of Revenue: county rates and reappraisal schedules](https://www.ncdor.gov/taxes-forms/property-tax/property-tax-rates/county-property-tax-rates-and-reappraisal-schedules) |
| South Carolina | Conventional occupancy and South Carolina legal residence | [South Carolina Department of Revenue: property exemption and legal-residence guidance](https://dor.sc.gov/property/exempt-property) |

[Fannie Mae, occupancy types](https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b2-1.1-01/occupancy-types) describes specific non-occupying borrower arrangements. Its parent-housing and adult-child provisions include circumstances involving inability to work or insufficient income to qualify independently. Relationship, documentation and the rest of mortgage underwriting still matter.

[Freddie Mac, Section 4201.11](https://guide.freddiemac.com/app/servicing/section/4201.11) provides its own primary-residence provisions for certain parent and disability-related family arrangements. Use the governing agency rule and lender requirements for the actual scenario. Do not transfer a Fannie Mae condition into Freddie Mac copy without checking its guide.

State homestead, exemption and classification rules are separate from agency mortgage occupancy. None of these references establishes automatic tax benefits merely because a mortgage is treated as a primary-residence loan.

## Limits of verification

Sources were found and checked with web research tools. Some references were supported by indexed excerpts rather than a complete rendered read of an interactive page. This register is not an exhaustive external-link uptime report, legal opinion or verification of all local rules. No unsupported lender matrix, city rent-growth statistic, credit threshold or current rate has been added. Source-based factual passages are paraphrased; no long source passages are reproduced.
