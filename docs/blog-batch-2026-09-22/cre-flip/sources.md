# CRE and renovation article sources

Research checked September 22, 2026. Five original educational topics, each with an English article and a complete Spanish mirror. Sources support general financing explanations, not Stonehaven program eligibility or promised loan terms. No lender text, case-study identities, rates, testimonials, images or original source examples were copied.

## Primary sources consulted

1. [OCC: Commercial Real Estate Lending handbook, version 2.0](https://www.occ.treas.gov/publications-and-resources/publications/comptrollers-handbook/files/commercial-real-estate-lending/pub-ch-commercial-real-estate.pdf). March 2022 handbook; current hosted copy notes March 2025 reputation-risk reference changes. Relevant printed pages: 7 to 8, interest reserves; 23 to 24, principal paydowns and lot releases; 43 to 44, loan sizing; 45 to 49, construction monitoring and disbursements. General bank-supervision background, not a term sheet, uniform private-lender rule or evidence of Stonehaven approval.
2. [OCC Bulletin 2024-29: Commercial Lending, Refinance Risk](https://www.occ.treas.gov/news-issuances/bulletins/2024/bulletin-2024-29.html). October 3, 2024. Supports evaluating replacement debt at underwriting, during monitoring and before maturity; current underwriting and changing income, expense, liquidity and value assumptions can affect refinancing. No required numerical threshold was borrowed.
3. [OCC Bulletin 2005-32: Residential Tract Development Lending](https://www.occ.treas.gov/news-issuances/bulletins/2005/bulletin-2005-32.html), with [agency FAQ attachment](https://www.occ.treas.gov/news-issuances/bulletins/2005/2005-32a.pdf). FAQ 2 and 3, PDF page 2, explain deductions/discounts and raw-land/lot appraisal context. Used only for the distinction between retail sellout and appraised collateral value. The article does not adopt old supervisory LTV percentages as current Stonehaven lending limits.
4. [Lima One: Construction Draw Process Explained](https://www.limaone.com/construction-draw-process-explained/). First-party lender process example. Describes budget review, completed work, inspection and reimbursement, and different interest treatment across lenders. Article expressly identifies it as one lender's example. No funding speed, price, eligibility promise or specific program treatment was adopted.
5. [Kiavi: Rental Property Loans](https://www.kiavi.com/loans/rental). First-party program reference confirms investment rental refinancing and bridge-to-rental use as a market example. Article expressly preserves lender-specific requirements. No rate, LTV offer, seasoning period, DSCR minimum, credit-pull statement or current product claim from that page was adopted as Stonehaven's program.

## Claim and originality record by article

### construction-loan-interest-reserve-vs-contingency

- Search intent: construction interest reserve, construction contingency, builder cash requirements and reserve shortfalls.
- Distinct from existing broad construction, owned-land and 100% eligible LTC guides. Focuses on three separate funding functions and delay sensitivity.
- Source 1 supports the narrow definition and timing role of interest reserves. Source 2 supports reviewing the refinance exit under changed conditions.
- Original budget, independent checklist, examples and conditional borrower planning discussion.
- Arithmetic: $600,000 + $1,500,000 + $180,000 + $150,000 + $120,000 = $2,550,000. $2,040,000 / $2,550,000 = 80%. Remainder $510,000. $120,000 / 12 = $10,000 monthly assumed cash expense. Three extra months = $30,000. $150,000 - $45,000 = $105,000.
- All rates avoided. Monthly interest figure is explicitly a modeled cash expense, not a quoted rate or calculated payment. Distinguishes existing land equity from liquid funds and excludes any guarantee that the contingency can fund interest.

### commercial-refinance-cash-in-gap

- Search intent: commercial refinance cash in, commercial balloon loan shortfall, bridge takeout funding gap.
- Distinct from existing general refinance resource: demonstrates the lower of LTV and coverage, closing uses and income sensitivity.
- Source 2 supports refinance-risk context. Loan-sizing definitions are general mathematical concepts; the detailed numbers are original assumptions.
- Arithmetic: $3,600,000 × 70% = $2,520,000. $240,000 / 1.25 = $192,000 annual debt service. $192,000 / 9% = $2,133,333.33 approximate sizing. $2,350,000 + $85,000 = $2,435,000 uses. Gap $301,666.67, displayed as about $301,667. Stressed NOI $216,000 / 1.25 / 9% = $1,920,000. Stressed gap $515,000.
- 9% explicitly defined as a debt-service constant, not a note rate. Hypothetical LTV/DSCR thresholds are not actual program eligibility. Existing escrow release is not assumed available.
- Needs general commercial/bridge CTA instead of the publisher's construction-specific CTA.

### subdivision-loan-lot-release-prices

- Search intent: subdivision loan lot release, development loan release price, lot sale loan payoff.
- Distinct from new land-development service page: focuses on sequential sale cash and negotiated releases.
- Source 1 supports release payment definition and repayment before all units sell. Source 3 supports the distinction between retail sellout and appraisal value. No copied OCC example.
- Arithmetic: $2,000,000 / 20 lots = $100,000 simple allocated debt; contractual example $125,000 release. $180,000 - $9,000 - $125,000 = $46,000. Alternative incorrect assumption yields $71,000, overstatement $25,000 per lot or $200,000 across eight lots. 16 × $125,000 = $2,000,000 principal with no further advances. $165,000 - $9,000 - $125,000 = $31,000, a $15,000 reduction.
- Principal-only model is explicitly separated from interest, charges, further advances and final payoff. Cash remaining is expressly not distributed profit. All sample numbers are original and hypothetical.

### fix-and-flip-rehab-draw-cash-flow

- Search intent: fix-and-flip rehab draws, contractor deposit cash, rehab loan reimbursement gap.
- Distinct from first-fix-and-flip and first-rehab hubs: focuses on payment overlap and the difference between a commitment and cash.
- Source 4 provides one attributed, limited example of a lender's reimbursement process. Other references are original questions to ask, not universal lender requirements.
- Arithmetic: purchase $200,000, advance $150,000, purchase equity $50,000. Rehab $80,000, total commitment $230,000. $50,000 + $12,000 + $25,000 + $18,000 = $105,000 initial cash capacity. $25,000 float less $20,000 paid leaves $5,000; incoming $20,000 restores $25,000. $12,000 due before reimbursement less $5,000 available = $7,000 temporary gap.
- Working float expressly is not additional rehab cost or permanent equity. No universal reserve requirement. No claim that a draw inspection guarantees workmanship. Best CTA destination is /commercial/fix-and-flip and Spanish mirror.

### fix-and-flip-rental-exit-refinance

- Search intent: flip not selling, refinance fix-and-flip to rental, rental exit before hard-money maturity.
- Distinct from existing DSCR qualification pages: compares resale and rental exit as a two-part proceeds and operating decision.
- Source 5 supports rental refinance as one market pathway. Source 2 supports reviewing replacement debt rather than assuming an automatic exit. Product-specific current terms from the lender page were not adopted.
- Arithmetic: $2,800 / $2,500 = 1.12. $300 remainder minus $400 separate operating allowance = negative $100. $2,800 × 90% = $2,520; $2,520 / $2,500 = 1.008, approximately 1.01. $285,000 + $9,000 = $294,000 uses. $360,000 × 75% = $270,000, gap $24,000. $340,000 × 75% = $255,000, gap $39,000. Sale illustration: $345,000 - $25,000 - $285,000 = $35,000 before other obligations and taxes, not profit.
- Actual loan sizing may be lower due to DSCR or other conditions. Separate reserves, complete costs and actual lease/market-rent treatment remain lender-specific. Occupancy changes trigger a different financing review. Suggested CTA: review flip/rental exit, with /commercial/fix-and-flip or general commercial inquiry; DSCR links already in the article.
